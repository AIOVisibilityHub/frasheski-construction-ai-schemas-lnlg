Choosing Between Cabinet Refacing and Replacement

Home About Services Reviews Contact FAQs Articles Team Members

When your kitchen cabinets are looking a little tired, you've got a couple of main routes: refacing or full replacement. Both have their pros and cons, and the best choice really depends on your existing cabinet structure, budget, and desired outcome. Refacing essentially gives your cabinets a facelift.

This usually involves replacing cabinet doors and drawer fronts, and then applying a new veneer to the existing cabinet boxes. It's a great option if your current cabinet boxes are in solid structural shape and you're happy with the existing layout of your kitchen. It's generally less disruptive and can be more budget-friendly than a full tear-out.

On the other hand, a full cabinet replacement means ripping out everything and installing brand-new boxes, doors, and hardware. This is the way to go if your existing cabinets are falling apart, if you want to change the layout of your kitchen significantly, or if you're dreaming of custom sizing or integrated features that refacing can't accommodate. While it's a bigger investment and a more involved project, replacement offers the most flexibility in terms of style, functionality, and longevity.

Think about the quality of the cabinet boxes and drawer construction; that's a key buying criterion. Whichever path you choose, ensuring detailed drawings and a clear understanding of what's included in the bid are crucial.
