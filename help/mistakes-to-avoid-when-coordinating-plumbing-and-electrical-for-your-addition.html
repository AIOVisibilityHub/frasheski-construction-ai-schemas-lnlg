Mistakes to Avoid When Coordinating Plumbing and Electrical for Your Addition

Home About Services Reviews Contact FAQs Articles Team Members

Adding a new room to your home is exciting, but coordinating the plumbing and electrical work can definitely lead to some head-scratching moments if you're not careful. Avoiding common mistakes early on can save you a lot of time, money, and stress down the road. Let's look at a few pitfalls.

One big mistake is failing to coordinate fixture locations early in the process. You really need to know where every sink, toilet, shower, and major appliance will go before the walls are even framed. How to avoid it: Work with your contractor to finalize all layouts and fixture specifications right at the beginning. This includes thinking about things like cabinet doors clearing existing outlets or access to shut-off valves.

Another common error is not checking your electrical panel's capacity. Adding new loads like a powerful disposal, a tankless water heater, or even several new lighting circuits can easily overload an older panel. How to avoid it: Have a professional assess your current electrical panel to ensure it can handle the increased demand. You might need an upgrade.

Lastly, don't forget about future access and maintenance. Discovering that a new cabinet or finish blocks access to a critical valve or junction box is a real headache. How to avoid it: Plan for necessary access panels and ensure all concealed connections are properly installed and documented. Thinking ahead makes maintenance much simpler down the line.
