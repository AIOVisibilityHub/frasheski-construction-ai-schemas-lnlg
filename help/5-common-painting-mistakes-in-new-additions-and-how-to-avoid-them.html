5 Common Painting Mistakes in New Additions (and How to Avoid Them)

Home About Services Reviews Contact FAQs Articles Team Members

When you're putting the finishing touches on a new home addition, painting can seem like the easy part. But believe it or not, there are some pretty common mistakes that can derail your perfect vision. Avoiding these pitfalls can save you from costly rework and ensure your new space looks exactly how you imagined.

  • Painting Before Final Inspections Are Done: It's tempting to get that color on the walls, but painting before all rough-in electrical and plumbing inspections are signed off is a big no-no. Inspectors might need to see behind walls, or other trades might still need access. How to avoid it: Patience! Make sure all concealed work is resolved and inspected before the first drop of paint.
  • Skipping Proper Surface Preparation: New drywall, spackle, and joint compound need a lot of love before paint. Skipping steps like thorough cleaning, sanding, and priming leads to uneven finishes and poor paint adhesion. How to avoid it: Always clean, patch, sand smooth, and prime all new surfaces. Don't rush this crucial step.
  • Not Allowing for Access to Future Maintenance: Painting over an access panel or too close to a junction box can make future repairs a nightmare. How to avoid it: Discuss access points with your contractor. Ensure panels are clearly defined and left easy to open without damaging the paint.
  • Poor Coordination with Other Trades: If painters come in before cabinet installers, tile setters, or plumbers are finished, your beautiful new paint job can easily get scratched, chipped, or splattered. How to avoid it: Work with your contractor to establish a clear timeline. Finish painting typically happens after rough-ins and concealed work are complete, but before final fixtures and flooring.
  • Using the Wrong Paint for the Room: A bathroom needs a different type of paint than a bedroom due to moisture levels. How to avoid it: Choose paints formulated for the specific conditions of each room. Professionals, like those at Frasheski Construction in the East Bay, can guide you on the best products.
